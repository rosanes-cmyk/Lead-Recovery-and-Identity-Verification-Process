# Lead Recovery and Identity Verification — Standard Operating Procedure

**Recommended task name:** Investigate and Verify Seller Identity and Contact Information

---

## 1. Purpose

The Lead Recovery and Identity Verification Project investigates stalled,
incomplete, or conflicting lead records. The objective is to:

- Confirm the correct property owner or seller name.
- Verify the connection between the person and the property.
- Locate accurate contact information.
- Identify conflicts in ownership or identity.
- Document all findings in REI BlackBook.
- Prepare the lead for proper reassignment and follow-up.

The investigator gathers, verifies, and documents information. The investigator
does **not** make pricing, negotiation, legal, fraud, or final business
decisions.

---

## 2. Roles and Responsibilities

### Lead Recovery Investigator

- Review the complete lead record.
- Research ownership and identity.
- Verify names, phone numbers, email addresses, and mailing addresses.
- Document sources, dates, and confidence levels.
- Identify conflicts or missing information.
- Recommend a final investigation status.
- Prepare the complete REI BlackBook note.
- Prepare the next task and reassignment recommendation.

### Manager or Authorized Operator

- Approve outreach.
- Approve contact with relatives or connected persons.
- Approve the final investigation status.
- Save the final note.
- Create the next task and due date.
- Reassign the lead.
- Make business, pricing, negotiation, or legal decisions.

---

## 3. Standing Rules

1. Do not negotiate with the seller.
2. Do not discuss pricing or make offers.
3. Do not provide legal advice.
4. Do not determine whether fraud occurred.
5. Do not invent statuses, dispositions, or workflow steps.
6. Use only approved statuses listed in the company SOP.
7. Do not contact anyone unless outreach is specifically authorized.
8. Do not contact relatives without separate authorization.
9. Do not save the final note without approval.
10. Do not change the task status without approval.
11. Do not create or reassign tasks without approval.
12. Do not collect information unrelated to the investigation objective.
13. Stop when the investigation time limit is reached.
14. Flag conflicting information instead of guessing which information is correct.
15. Treat people-search information as a research lead, not as official proof.

---

## 4. Approved Research Sources

- REI BlackBook
- Existing company records
- County Assessor
- County Recorder
- County Tax Collector
- County GIS or parcel viewer
- Superior Court or probate records, when relevant
- PropertyRadar
- DealMachine
- Google
- Lawful public records
- Company-approved people-search tools

### People-Search Tool Restriction

People-search tools such as TruePeopleSearch, Spokeo, or similar services may
only be used when:

- The company has approved the tool.
- The investigation has a legitimate business purpose.
- The search is limited to identifying or verifying the seller.
- The information is cross-checked against stronger sources.
- The investigator does not collect unnecessary private information.

**People-search results alone must never be treated as proof of ownership.**

---

## 5. Investigation Time Limits

### Standard Investigation — max 30 minutes

Use for:

- Basic owner verification.
- Phone or email verification.
- Mailing-address research.
- Simple name mismatches.
- Standard stalled-lead investigation.

### Enhanced Investigation — max 60 minutes (requires manager authorization)

Use for:

- Multiple possible owners.
- Trust or estate ownership.
- Probate concerns.
- Major identity conflicts.
- Several disconnected phone numbers or addresses.
- Complex ownership history.

When the time limit is reached, **stop the investigation, document all findings,
and recommend the appropriate status.**

---

## Complete Investigation Workflow

### Step 1 — Review the Existing Lead Record

Open the lead in REI BlackBook and review all available information:

- Seller name, property address, mailing address
- Phone numbers, email addresses
- Property owner information
- Lead source, notes, call summaries, text messages, emails, attachments
- Previous tasks, last activity, last disposition
- Assigned team member, property status, existing ownership information

Prepare a short **baseline summary**:

- Name currently listed
- Phone numbers on file
- Email address on file
- Property address
- Mailing address
- Last contact date
- Last disposition
- Current lead stage
- Current assigned team member
- Conflicts or red flags

**Do not begin outreach during this step.**

### Step 2 — Confirm the Investigation Objective

Before additional research, clearly identify:

- Priority level
- Investigation bucket
- Seller name
- Property address
- Exact research objective
- Standard or Enhanced investigation
- Whether outreach is authorized
- Whether alternate contacts may be researched
- Whether relatives may be researched or contacted
- Any special scope limitations

**Example objective:** "Verify the correct owner name for 123 Main Street,
confirm whether the phone number ending in 1234 belongs to the owner, and locate
the owner's current mailing address."

> Outreach is never assumed. If authorization is unclear, **stop before
> contacting anyone.**

### Step 3 — Verify Property Ownership

Research ownership using the strongest available sources first:

1. County Assessor
2. County Recorder
3. County Tax Collector
4. County GIS or parcel viewer
5. PropertyRadar
6. DealMachine
7. Other lawful public records

Record:

- Owner name exactly as displayed
- Ownership type
- Vesting information
- Trust or entity name
- Mailing address of record
- Recording date, when available
- Document number, when available
- Source
- Date checked

> **Important:** A people-search website cannot confirm property ownership.
> Ownership must be verified through county records, recorded documents, or
> another reliable property-data source.

### Step 4 — Use People Search to Identify the Correct Name

Use people search **only after** reviewing the company record and available
property information. Its purpose is to:

- Identify the likely full name connected to a phone number.
- Identify the likely person connected to an address.
- Resolve spelling differences.
- Find a possible middle name or initial.
- Determine whether a contact record may belong to the seller.
- Generate research leads for further verification.

It must **not** be the only source for ownership confirmation.

#### Method 1 — Search by Property Address

Use when the seller's name is unknown, incomplete, or possibly incorrect.

1. Open the approved people-search website.
2. Select the address-search option.
3. Enter the complete property address (street number, street name, city, state, ZIP).
4. Review listed current and previous residents.
5. Write down only names reasonably connected to the property.
6. Compare names against county ownership records, PropertyRadar, DealMachine, and existing REI BlackBook information.
7. Do not assume every resident is an owner.
8. Do not automatically classify people with the same last name as relatives.

Record: name displayed, address searched, current/previous, source, date
checked, matching information, confidence level.

#### Method 2 — Search by Phone Number

Use when the lead has a phone number but the associated name is missing or questionable.

1. Select reverse-phone search.
2. Enter the complete phone number.
3. Review the associated name.
4. Compare against the seller name, county owner name, mailing address, property address, and PropertyRadar information.
5. Check for spelling differences, middle names, initials, or alternate last names.
6. Do not assume the phone belongs to the owner based on one result.
7. Verify the name through at least one additional source.

**Example**

| Source | Value |
| --- | --- |
| REI BlackBook name | Philip Barber |
| People-search result | Phillip Lyman Barber |
| County record | Barber Phillip Lyman |

Conclusion: the identity may be a strong match — first and last names align, the
spelling difference is minor, and the middle name appears in official ownership
records.

#### Method 3 — Search by Name

Use when the seller's name is available but the correct address or phone is uncertain.

1. Search the person's full name.
2. Add city and state whenever possible.
3. Use a middle name or initial when available.
4. Review possible results.
5. Compare each result against property address, mailing address, county owner information, known phone number, known email address.
6. Eliminate results that clearly belong to another location or person.
7. Do not collect information on unrelated individuals.

Search order:

1. Full legal name
2. Full name plus city
3. Full name plus state
4. First and last name plus property address
5. First and last name plus known phone number
6. Alternate spelling or recorded middle name

#### Method 4 — Search by Email

Use only when the approved people-search tool supports reverse-email lookup.

1. Enter the complete email address.
2. Review the associated name.
3. Compare with seller name, phone number, mailing address, property address, county owner information.
4. Record the result as a possible identity match.
5. Do not treat the email result as official ownership proof.

### Step 5 — Determine the Correct Name

Select the correct name based on the strongest combination of matching information.

**Strong matching factors**

- Name matches county owner records.
- Property address matches.
- Mailing address matches.
- Phone number connects to the same name.
- Email connects to the same name.
- Middle name or initial matches recorded documents.
- Multiple independent sources show the same identity.
- The seller previously confirmed the information.

**Weak matching factors**

- Same last name only.
- Same city only.
- Old address only.
- One people-search result with no official match.
- Similar age range.
- A listed possible relative.
- A social-media profile with no property connection.

**Name formatting rule:** Use the most complete name supported by the strongest
source.

> County record: `Phillip Lyman Barber` · REI BlackBook: `Philip Barber` →
> Recommended verified name: **`Phillip Lyman Barber`**

Add a note explaining the original spelling variation. **Do not silently replace
the name without documenting why.**

### Step 6 — Verify the Mailing Address

Start with the county owner mailing address, then compare with PropertyRadar,
DealMachine, people-search results, recorded deeds, tax records, and existing
company records.

Confirm whether the mailing address is:

- The property address
- A separate owner mailing address
- A previous address
- A possible forwarding address
- Unconfirmed

> Do not recommend sending a gift, letter, or package until the correct mailing
> address is confirmed.

### Step 7 — Verify the Best Phone Number

For each number, record: phone number, associated name, source, date checked,
whether it matches the seller, whether it matches the property, whether it is
current/possible/unverified, and confidence level.

**Phone classification**

- **Confirmed** — the seller directly confirmed the number, or several reliable sources connect it to the owner.
- **Likely** — connected to the correct name and matching location, but not seller-confirmed.
- **Possible** — appears in one source but lacks enough supporting information.
- **Invalid or Unrelated** — connected to another person or confirmed wrong.

### Step 8 — Verify the Email Address

For each email address, record: email address, associated name, source, date
checked, match to the seller, match to the property, confidence level.

> Do not assume an email belongs to the owner solely because it appears in a
> people-search result.

### Step 9 — Handle Possible Relatives and Connected Persons

A person may only be described as a **verified** relative or legal connection
when a lawful and reliable source documents the relationship, for example:

- Named co-owner on a recorded deed
- Grantor or grantee on a recorded document
- Trustee or beneficiary named in a lawful record
- Personal representative named in a probate record
- Spouse explicitly listed in a public ownership record

The following are **not** enough to establish a relationship:

- Same last name
- Same address
- People-search "possible relative" listing
- Social-media connection
- Similar age
- Nearby residence

Do not collect unnecessary private contact information about possible relatives.
Do not contact a relative or connected person without explicit authorization.
When a possible family or estate issue is discovered, recommend **Management
Review Required.**

### Step 10 — Assign a Confidence Level

Every important finding must have one confidence level.

- **High** — supported by county records, recorded deeds, court records, direct seller confirmation, or multiple matching reliable sources.
- **Medium** — likely correct; several details match; official confirmation still missing.
- **Low** — only one weak source; a possible match; conflicting information remains; or the result comes only from a people-search aggregator.

**Low-confidence information must not be treated as verified.**

### Step 11 — Authorized Outreach

Outreach may only occur when authorization was confirmed in Step 2. Normal
outreach order:

1. Call
2. Voicemail
3. Text message
4. Email
5. Alternate number
6. Letter
7. Relative or connected-person callback — **only when separately authorized**

The investigator prepares the draft and confirms the correct contact
information. The investigator does **not**: negotiate, discuss offer amounts,
discuss property pricing, make promises, provide legal conclusions, accuse anyone
of fraud, or contact relatives without permission.

### Step 12 — Document Every Finding

Every finding must include: information found, source, date checked, confidence
level, matching evidence, conflicting evidence, and whether further verification
is required.

**Example**

> Phone: (925) 555-1234 · Associated name: Phillip Lyman Barber · Source:
> Approved reverse-phone search · Date checked: July 16, 2026 · Confidence:
> Medium · Reason: Name matches the recorded property owner, but the number has
> not been confirmed directly by the seller.

### Step 13 — Recommend One Final Status

Select **only one** status from the approved SOP list.

- **Seller Contact Located** — the correct seller/owner has been identified, and at least one usable contact method or mailing address has been confirmed.
- **Seller Confirmed Interested** — outreach was authorized, the correct seller responded, and the seller clearly confirmed continued interest.
- **No Contact After Investigation** — the authorized time limit was reached, no reliable contact method was confirmed, and the seller could not be located.
- **Management Review Required** — ownership conflicts exist; seller identity is uncertain; a trust/estate/probate/fraud concern appears; several possible owners remain; a possible family connection requires approval; or the lead information materially conflicts with official records.

### Step 14 — Prepare the Next Task

The final note must include a clear next action containing: action required,
assigned person or department, due date, contact method (when applicable),
verified contact information, reason for the task, and any restrictions or
warnings.

**Example**

> Next Task: Acquisitions to call the verified seller at (925) 555-1234. Due
> Date: July 17, 2026. Reason: Phone number is connected to the recorded property
> owner with Medium confidence. Seller confirmation is still required.

### Step 15 — Reassign the Lead

After the complete investigation note is approved and saved:

- **Reassign to Acquisitions** — seller contact information has been located, the mailing address has been confirmed, and the lead is ready for seller follow-up.
- **Reassign to Cherry Hombre** — status is Management Review Required; ownership or identity conflicts remain; a trust/estate/probate/possible-relative issue exists; or management approval is required.
- **Reassign to Transaction Coordination** — only when the lead is already under contract and the investigation affects an active transaction.

---

## Guardrail

This investigation provides research and identity-verification information only.
No pricing, negotiation, fraud determination, legal conclusion, outreach, task
change, note saving, or reassignment is completed without authorization.

## Definition of Done

The investigation is not complete until all four actions have been completed:

1. The complete investigation note is saved in REI BlackBook.
2. The approved final status is selected.
3. The next task and due date are created.
4. The lead is reassigned to the correct person or department.

The investigator **prepares and recommends** these actions; the authorized
operator **approves and executes** them. A lead must not be declared complete
while any of the four actions remain outstanding.
